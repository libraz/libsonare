# Voicematch measurements

The measurement contract is the data emitted by [`metrics.py`](../metrics.py), [`metrics_hit.py`](../metrics_hit.py), and [`metrics_texture.py`](../metrics_texture.py). Shape measurements use the mono normalized render; raw held-level fields are present only when a raw render is supplied. Run commands from the repository root with `rye run --pyproject bindings/python/pyproject.toml python ...`; use each command's `--help` for the complete option set.

## Pitched measurements

### Fields

| group | current fields | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| identity | `note`, `velocity` | Keyboard note and velocity axes attached to each row. |
| pitch and partials | `f0_hz`, `f0_cents_err`, `harmonics_db`, `modal_hz`, `modal_db`, `modal_ratio`, `ladder_partials`, `inharmonicity_b`, `inharmonicity_partials` | Fundamental, harmonic or measured modal partials, and inharmonicity. |
| spectrum | `centroid_hz`, `odd_even_db`, `tnr_db` | Spectral shape and tonal-to-noise measures; `centroid_hz` is reported but is not a pitched loss term. |
| envelope | `attack_ms`, `attack_fine_ms`, `onset_ms`, `onset_overshoot_db`, `onset_settle_ms`, `sustain_slope_db_s`, `release_ms`, `release_capped`, `sustain_rms_db`, `skeleton` | The score-anchored attack plus finer onset and envelope probes. `onset_overshoot_db` is the envelope's peak in the first 150 ms over the 95th percentile of the sustain level, and `onset_settle_ms` the time until it stays within 1 dB plus the sustain's own tremolo depth of that level. The sustain is the first of 0.2–0.6 s and 0.6–1.2 s whose slope is within ±4 dB/s, and its level is read on that window's first half, so a slow wind overshoot settles into the later window and a swell reads negative. Both fields are absent where neither window is held, so a decaying note has neither. `skeleton` contains `init_db`, `early_db_s`, `late_db_s`, and `tail_db_s`. |
| attack shape | `attack_hf_db`, `attack_lf_db`, `attack_peaks` | High/low attack balance and detected attack peaks. |
| modulation | `f0_width_cents`, `vib_cents`, `vib_rate_hz`, `trem_db`, `trem_rate_hz`, `beat_db`, `beat_rate_hz`, `fm_slow_cents`, `fm_slow_rate_hz`, `fm_fast_cents`, `fm_fast_rate_hz` | Pitch, amplitude, and beating movement. Every pitch reading, `vib_*` in the 3-9 Hz band and the `fm_*` pair outside it (1-3 Hz and 9-45 Hz), is taken off one demodulated fundamental whose lowpass stays under 0.45 f0, so a still note reads no vibrato down to the bottom of the piano. All four are absent where the fundamental's passband does not stand 18 dB over the floor within f0 of it (noise, drums, a missing fundamental), since that phase is the floor's; the level readings come from a 50 ms frame tracker. |
| raw level | `peak_dbfs`, `held_rms_dbfs`, `held_crest_db` | Absolute peak, held level, and held crest factor when raw audio is available; shape rows remain normalized. |

`tnr_db` counts the energy inside a window around every partial up to 12 kHz as tone. When the held segment carries a vibrato, each window widens by the vibrato's FM sidebands down to −60 dB (capped at a quarter of f0), so a vibrato note reads as clean as the same note held still. The vibrato is read by `metrics_modulation.vibrato_extent` off the demodulated fundamental. Its 3–9 Hz peak must stand above both neighbouring bins and sit at least three cycles into the track, and then either its line holds half the track's modulation energy (a periodic vibrato) or the whole band holds 70 % of the track below 18 Hz at 3 cents or more (a player's vibrato whose rate wanders, or whose line shares the track with fast FM from another component); slow drift, unison beating and a decay cut to a quarter second fail both. Either shape is read as the band's RMS depth over the rates within 10 dB of its peak, and a wandering rate's mask spaces the slowest rate's sideband order at the fastest rate.

Where no vibrato is found, `metrics_modulation.pitch_excursion` reads how far the fundamental's pitch moves whatever its periodicity — a section's or a choir's aperiodic churn, players beating against each other — and the mask widens by that reading through the same sideband arithmetic, at one rate. Only the held part of the track is read: the first 0.3 s and the last 0.15 s are dropped, so a struck or plucked note's onset glide and a release's fall are not taken for motion, and a held part under 40 steps (0.2 s) reads none. The depth is twice the 99th percentile of the power-weighted distance from the mean pitch, the rate is where nine tenths of the motion's level-weighted power lies, and the track rolls off from 9 Hz to zero at 18 Hz under a demodulation passband that stops at a quarter of f0 (the mask's own cap, short of a layer a fourth below). A drift or a glide whose rate completes fewer than three cycles over the track is not an excursion: within ±34 cents it stays inside each partial's still window, and widening for it only masks the analysis window's own leakage on the side that drifts. The excursion is also absent under 10 cents peak to peak, the phase a tremolo's or a beat's sidebands leak into a fundamental, where it reaches past the passband, and where its index (peak deviation over rate at the fundamental) is under 0.05 — the index a component 26 dB down beside the fundamental beats with, which is another component rather than the note spreading. `metrics_partials.pitch_motion` returns the vibrato where there is one and the excursion otherwise, so a vibrato reads as it did. Players whose vibratos cancel in the mean pitch read shallower than each one moves.

The same motion reading decides the pitch and the partial stack, in the fit's `analyze_note` and the reference profile's `find_partials` alike. Under a pitch motion the spectral peak is whichever FM line won and the upper partials' peaks fall with the modulation index, so under a vibrato the fundamental (`f0_hz`, `f0_cents_err`, `cents_vs_et`) is the mean pitch of the fundamental demodulated under its own FM extent (a wider passband lets a component beside the fundamental pull the mean). An excursion leaves the fundamental at its spectral peak: it has no line for the peak to have picked off-centre, and where it is two components beating the demodulated mean moves off the stronger one. Under either motion each partial level (`harmonics_db`, `partials_db`) is its power summed over the same FM reach the tone mask uses. A still tone keeps the peak readings unchanged.

The independent profile axes are [`ToneClass`](../toneclass.py), [`ExcitationFamily`](../toneclass.py), and the keyboard interface. A `FitProfile` combines those axes; it does not infer one axis from another. The current classes are `sustained`, `struck-string`, `plucked-string`, `modal`, and `noise`; the excitation families are `bowed`, `plucked`, `hammered`, `struck-modal`, `percussion`, `breath-onset` (the free-reed, brass and flute engines, whose breath or bellows onset the engine shapes), and `other`.

Stringlike classes may use a harmonic ladder and stiffness; modal classes use measured modes found in the signal. A modal voice has no harmonic ladder requirement. `modal_hz` and `modal_db` describe measured partials, and the `modes` term matches those partials when the reference provides them. `ToneClass.stringlike` also decides the gate: the four dimensions read off the n·f0 ladder (`stretch`, `balance`, `partial_tilt`, `tnr`) are canonical only for a stringlike class, so a modal capture neither asks for them nor holds a bound on them, and its `dimensions_na` records what each would read on that reference.

Pitch and envelope windows are bounded by the analyzed note duration. `attack_ms` remains score anchored; fine attack and modulation fields are additional probes. A silent or non-comparable row is unscorable rather than a zero measurement.

## Percussion measurements

### Fields

| group | current fields | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| identity and bands | `note`, `velocity`, `bands_db`, `peak_band_hz`, `band_decay_db_s` | Hit coordinates, loudness-normalized one-third-octave bands, peak band, and octave-band decay rates. |
| onset and envelope | `onset_ms`, `attack_ms`, `attack_floored`, `decay_ms`, `decay_capped`, `crest_db`, `level_db` | Transient timing, floor/cap provenance, crest, and normalized hit level. |
| spectral shape | `centroid_hz`, `flatness_db`, `tilt_rise_db`, `tilt_strike_db` | Spectrum, flatness, and body-versus-strike tilt; `bright` is a loss term derived from the measured spectrum. |
| tone and modes | `tone_f0_hz`, `tone_lowest_hz`, `modal_hz`, `modal_db`, `modal_ratio`, `pitch_drop_ratio`, `pitch_drop_ms` | Strongest/lowest mode and mode ratios; pitch drop is measured data and may be represented through the mode terms. `tonal` is a loss term derived from flatness. |
| stereo and texture | `stereo_width`, `modal_density`, `prompt_late_db`, `evolution_db`, `window_flatness_db` | Stereo width, octave-cell density/prompt measures, and early spectral evolution. |
| masks and diagnostics | `modal_density_valid`, `prompt_late_valid`, `evolution_valid`, `evolution_measured`, `window_flatness_valid`, `window_flatness_measured` | Domain eligibility and finite geometry validity. Genuinely unmeasured slots use placeholders; below-floor measured slots retain finite values even when reference eligibility is false. `ring` is a loss derived from decay. |

The ordinary percussion profile uses one-third-octave centers from 50 Hz through 12.5 kHz and octave decay centers from 63 Hz through 8 kHz. `band_edge_hz` is capture/profile metadata, not a hit-row field. A reference-derived band edge and the measured geometry determine which cells can participate; the profile does not promise energy beyond that edge.

`evolve` uses three windows and eight bands (20–60 Hz plus the seven texture bands) over a common untapered 0–240 ms span: 0–15 ms, 15–60 ms, and 60–240 ms; `diffuse` uses the same windows for 1–8 kHz window flatness. The first window uses a falling half-Hann; later windows use Hann. Power is Parseval-normalized and bandwidth-limited. Actual unpadded FFT bins control eligibility; zero padding interpolates the spectrum and does not resolve slow, low-frequency bands. A low band also needs two cycles and two actual bins.

The relative `-60 dB` threshold is a reference-domain eligibility rule, not a measured SNR. `*_valid` identifies reference-eligible cells; `*_measured` identifies finite candidate geometry even below that floor. A finite candidate below the reference floor is compared, while an absent candidate is charged by the loss contract. This prevents a threshold cliff.

## Loss and weights

### Terms

| scope | current terms |
| --- | --- |
| shared | `env`, `mss`, `level`, `crest`, `dyn`, `modes`, `bright` |
| pitched | `harm`, `cents`, `tnr`, `mod`, `init`, `slope`, `tail`, `hf`, `lf`, `stiff`, `hfdyn`, `modedyn`, `onsetvel`, `velspan`, `onset`, `arrival`, `body` |
| percussion | `band`, `bdecay`, `tilt`, `tonal`, `rise`, `strike`, `ring`, `lf`, `kit`, `density`, `prompt`, `evolve`, `diffuse` |

`bright` is the gate's centroid ratio, |100 (model / reference − 1)| per note, counted in `bright_hits`. It weighs 1 on percussion and 0 on a pitched voice, where breath and bow noise move it note by note; a pitched fit asks for it with `--w-bright`, which is what sees a string that has slipped into a higher regime — a missing fundamental in the bass costs `harm` little under audibility weighting and doubles the centroid.

`arrival` is the gate's `attack` dimension per note: |model − reference| `arrival_ms`, the time from the onset to the first point within 3 dB of the note's own maximum, read over the note to its note-off, counted in `arrival_hits` and scaled by 40 ms. `env` reads its attack as a 10–90 % rise inside the first half-second, which a level still climbing after that does not move; a string that creeps up 3 dB over two seconds matches `env` and arrives 650 ms late on the gate. It weighs 0 by default; `--w-arrival` opts in.

`body` is the gate's `body` dimension per note: |model − reference| `body_below_f0_db`, the 40 Hz to 0.7 f0 band against the fundamental's, from 0.2 to 1.2 s after the onset and only from a 120 Hz fundamental up, capped at 24 dB and counted in `body_hits`. A string bowed under its minimum force slips more than once a period and loses its fundamental, which reads tens of dB here while `harm` prices it lightly. It weighs 0 by default; `--w-body` opts in.

`dyn`, `hfdyn` and `modedyn` share one reducer, a per-pitch slope of a brightness reading against velocity, and differ in the reading: `dyn` the held ladder's h4–h10, `hfdyn` the attack's 4–8 kHz share, `modedyn` the share of measured mode power at 1.75× the played note and above, floored at the mode detector's 55 dB. `onsetvel` takes the same per-pitch slope of `attack_fine_ms` and `onset_overshoot_db` instead, so it reads how the onset changes with velocity; it weighs 0 by default, and a reference whose velocity rows share one onset cannot answer it. `velspan` is the term for the gate's `vel_range`: per pitch, the absolute difference between the model's and the reference's softest-to-hardest `peak_dbfs` span over the velocities both hold, capped at the level cap, with a model span missing where the reference has one charged the cap. It weighs 0.5 by default in the classes that default `dyn` or `modedyn`. The gate's `onset` and `onset_settle` dimensions (model minus reference `onset_overshoot_db` and `onset_settle_ms`, rows absent on either side skipped) are read by the `onset` term. `onset` compares the onset itself cell by cell: the overshoot's difference in dB plus the settle's in doublings, with the settle offset by 10 ms so a zero settle stays finite. A cell either side reads onset-ineligible is left out and counted by side (`onset_ref_ineligible`, `onset_model_ineligible`), never scored as a match. It weighs 1 for the `breath-onset` family and 0 otherwise, and it is dropped, or refused when named, on a capture whose committed profile has no eligible cell on the probe's grid. The modal class weights `modedyn` and `mod` in place of `dyn` and `tnr`; `mod` tracks the fundamental, so it reads a vibraphone's vanes and a beating pair at the fundamental, not a pair among the upper modes.

The authoritative term definitions, caps, and reducers are in [`loss.py`](../loss.py), [`loss_dimensions.py`](../loss_dimensions.py), and [`loss_weights.py`](../loss_weights.py). Losses use fixed engineering units recorded by `TERM_UNITS`, not perceptual units: pitch is cents, spectral and level residuals are dB-like engineering quantities, envelope and decay terms retain their declared time/rate units, and texture terms retain their cell residual units. One quality unit means one declared engineering unit for the aggregate term; it does not mean perceptual equivalence.

Pitched harmonic, modal, pitch, noise, envelope, and modulation terms compare the finite cells appropriate to their measurement. Percussion band, decay, tilt, tone, ring, kit, density, prompt, evolution, and diffuse terms use their corresponding cells. `mss` compares the whole timeline. Level terms first remove the median model/reference gain offset, so a common gain does not turn normalized shape agreement into an absolute-level claim. Raw held levels are scored only when both sides provide the raw fields.

Evolution and diffuse reducers retain the cell mean and the worst-quarter cell mean, with at least one cell in the quarter; the aggregate keeps the larger residual. The robust grid reducer separately reports mean, upper-tail, worst row, and note/velocity provenance. It does not report or independently gate every individual cell. Grid terms such as `mss`, `dyn`, `hfdyn`, and `kit` keep their declared grid reducer.

Reference-unknown data supplies no target. Fixed reference-cell reducers charge missing candidates at the cap; other count-tracked terms protect losses against disappearing measurements through anchored coverage penalties in `LossWeights`. `CellCount` records compared, clipped, absent, and skipped cells; `<term>_capped` includes clipped plus absent cells. For evolution and diffuse, ineligible reference cells remain outside the denominator and are reported separately as `evolution_reference_unavailable` and `window_flatness_reference_unavailable`. They do not increment `<term>_skipped`.

Weights are resolved in this order: explicit CLI weight, specification weight, then the `FitProfile`/class defaults in [`toneclass.py`](../toneclass.py) and [`loss_weights.py`](../loss_weights.py). The exact resolved values belong in the current fit/report output; this page does not copy a default numeric weight table. A normalized loss is relative to its starting weighted score; `--raw-loss` keeps the raw weighted sum. Neither display is a perceptual score.

### Quality units

[`fit_quality.py`](../fit_quality.py) reports absolute weighted residuals in the fixed term units and uses one declared unit as the default target for each aggregate and each reported worst row/condition. A term meets that numerical target only with complete known coverage and both residuals at most one declared unit. Unknown or zero anchored counts, lost measurements, and capped, absent, or skipped evidence prevent complete known coverage. Use [`report.py`](../report.py) for resolved weights and quality fields; [Acceptance](acceptance.md#numerical-quality) defines how to read them.

## Coverage

Coverage is assessed against the anchored reference domain and counts. Missing candidate geometry stays charged and does not shrink that domain. Pitched modal and harmonic domains are independent: a missing modal reference does not invent a harmonic target, and a modal candidate is not judged by a harmonic ladder. Percussion edge and texture masks distinguish reference eligibility from finite measured candidate data.

Fit quality must identify the active terms, compared/capped/absent/skipped counts, aggregate residual, worst row/condition, and whether all required dimensions were measured. Use [`reference.md`](reference.md) for capture and probe procedures, [`fitting.md`](fitting.md) for the CLI workflow, and [`development.md`](development.md) for recovery and probe work; those pages own procedures rather than this measurement contract.
