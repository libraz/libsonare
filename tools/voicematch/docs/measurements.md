# Voicematch measurements

The measurement contract is the data emitted by [`metrics.py`](../metrics.py), [`metrics_hit.py`](../metrics_hit.py), and [`metrics_texture.py`](../metrics_texture.py). Shape measurements use the mono normalized render; raw held-level fields are present only when a raw render is supplied. Run commands from the repository root with `uv run --locked --project bindings/python python ...`; use each command's `--help` for the complete option set.

## Pitched measurements

### Fields

| group | current fields | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| identity | `note`, `velocity` | Keyboard note and velocity axes attached to each row. |
| pitch and partials | `f0_hz`, `f0_cents_err`, `harmonics_db`, `modal_hz`, `modal_db`, `modal_ratio`, `ladder_partials`, `inharmonicity_b`, `inharmonicity_partials` | Fundamental, harmonic or measured modal partials, and inharmonicity. |
| spectrum | `centroid_hz`, `odd_even_db`, `tnr_db` | Spectral shape and tonal-to-noise measures over the sustain window; no pitched loss term reads them, `bright` and `tnr` read the fit window's (below). |
| gate readings | `cents_vs_et`, `fit_centroid_hz`, `fit_tnr_db`, `fit_tnr_mask`, `body_below_f0_db`, `held_peak_dbfs`, `mono_peak_dbfs`, `a4_offset_c` | The gate's own readings of the note, each through the function `profile_measure.measure_note` uses: off its fit window (`fit_window_readings`: the fundamental against equal temperament, the line-masked centroid, the tone-to-noise ratio through the reference's fit-window mask, which a reference row records as `fit_tnr_mask`) and its onset-anchored gate at the fit's f0 (the body under the fundamental, which replaces `analyze_note`'s own reading and is absent where the gate reads none), the onset-anchored gate's loudest 10 ms RMS (`held_peak_dbfs`, needs raw audio) and the largest sample of the un-normalised downmix from the note-on (`mono_peak_dbfs`, needs raw audio). | envelope | `attack_ms`, `attack_fine_ms`, `onset_ms`, `onset_overshoot_db`, `onset_settle_ms`, `sustain_slope_db_s`, `release_ms`, `release_capped`, `sustain_rms_db`, `skeleton` | The score-anchored attack plus finer onset and envelope probes. `onset_overshoot_db` is the envelope's peak in the first 150 ms over the 95th percentile of the sustain level, and `onset_settle_ms` the time until it stays within 1 dB plus the sustain's own tremolo depth of that level. The sustain is the first of 0.2–0.6 s and 0.6–1.2 s whose slope is within ±4 dB/s, and its level is read on that window's first half, so a slow wind overshoot settles into the later window and a swell reads negative. Both fields are absent where neither window is held, so a decaying note has neither. `skeleton` contains `init_db`, `early_db_s`, `late_db_s`, and `tail_db_s`. |
| attack shape | `attack_hf_db`, `attack_lf_db`, `attack_peaks` | High/low attack balance and detected attack peaks. |
| modulation | `f0_width_cents`, `vib_cents`, `vib_rate_hz`, `trem_db`, `trem_rate_hz`, `beat_db`, `beat_rate_hz`, `fm_slow_cents`, `fm_slow_rate_hz`, `fm_fast_cents`, `fm_fast_rate_hz` | Pitch, amplitude, and beating movement. Every pitch reading, `vib_*` in the 3-9 Hz band and the `fm_*` pair outside it (1-3 Hz and 9-45 Hz), is taken off one demodulated fundamental whose lowpass stays under 0.45 f0, so a still note reads no vibrato down to the bottom of the piano. All four are absent where the fundamental's passband does not stand 18 dB over the floor within f0 of it (noise, drums, a missing fundamental), since that phase is the floor's; the level readings come from a 50 ms frame tracker. |
| raw level | `peak_dbfs`, `held_rms_dbfs`, `held_crest_db` | Absolute peak (largest sample over the channels), held level, and held crest factor when raw audio is available; shape rows remain normalized. |

`tnr_db` counts the energy inside a window around every partial up to 12 kHz as tone. When the held segment carries a vibrato, each window widens by the vibrato's FM sidebands down to −60 dB (capped at a quarter of f0), so a vibrato note reads as clean as the same note held still. The vibrato is read by `metrics_modulation.vibrato_extent` off the demodulated fundamental. Its 3–9 Hz peak must stand above both neighbouring bins and sit at least three cycles into the track, and then either its line holds half the track's modulation energy (a periodic vibrato) or the whole band holds 70 % of the track below 18 Hz at 3 cents or more (a player's vibrato whose rate wanders, or whose line shares the track with fast FM from another component); slow drift, unison beating and a decay cut to a quarter second fail both. Either shape is read as the band's RMS depth over the rates within 10 dB of its peak, and a wandering rate's mask spaces the slowest rate's sideband order at the fastest rate.

Where no vibrato is found, `metrics_modulation.pitch_excursion` reads how far the fundamental's pitch moves whatever its periodicity — a section's or a choir's aperiodic churn, players beating against each other — and the mask widens by that reading through the same sideband arithmetic, at one rate. Only the held part of the track is read: the first 0.3 s and the last 0.15 s are dropped, so a struck or plucked note's onset glide and a release's fall are not taken for motion, and a held part under 40 steps (0.2 s) reads none. The depth is twice the 99th percentile of the power-weighted distance from the mean pitch, the rate is where nine tenths of the motion's level-weighted power lies, and the track rolls off from 9 Hz to zero at 18 Hz under a demodulation passband that stops at a quarter of f0 (the mask's own cap, short of a layer a fourth below). A drift or a glide whose rate completes fewer than three cycles over the track is not an excursion: within ±34 cents it stays inside each partial's still window, and widening for it only masks the analysis window's own leakage on the side that drifts. The excursion is also absent under 10 cents peak to peak, the phase a tremolo's or a beat's sidebands leak into a fundamental, where it reaches past the passband, and where its index (peak deviation over rate at the fundamental) is under 0.05 — the index a component 26 dB down beside the fundamental beats with, which is another component rather than the note spreading. `metrics_partials.pitch_motion` returns the vibrato where there is one and the excursion otherwise, so a vibrato reads as it did. Players whose vibratos cancel in the mean pitch read shallower than each one moves.

A periodic tremolo widens the mask as well, since a level swing puts sidebands at multiples of its rate around every partial. `metrics_modulation.tremolo_extent` reads it off the fundamental's level track (the same 50 ms frame tracker, linearly detrended so a decay reads none) over the segment the spectrum is taken from. The strongest 3–8 Hz bin must stand above both neighbouring bins, sit at least three cycles into the track and, with its neighbours, hold half the track's modulation energy above DC, at 1 dB peak to peak or more; an onset or the knee of a decay spreads over the low bins and holds no line. The swing is taken as a sinusoid in dB, whose sidebands are modified Bessel functions of the depth, so each window widens by the rate times the highest sideband order down to −60 dB, capped at a quarter of f0. A vibraphone's motor tremolo, about 6 dB at 4.3 Hz with its first pair at −16 dB, keeps two orders, which at the bottom of the bar lie outside the still window. Where a tremolo and a pitch motion are both read, the window reaches the farther of the two, since a level swing riding a vibrato is mostly the vibrato's own through the body's resonances. A note with no tremolo keeps its mask unchanged, and the sidebands left under −60 dB cap a tremolo note's ratio near 60 dB.

The mask is the reference's on both sides. A reference row records what widened its own mask as `tnr_mask` — `fm` holds the pitch motion's peak-to-peak cents and rate span, `am` the tremolo's depth and rate, each absent where the note held still — and the reference's ratio is read through that rounded record. A model note is measured through its reference row's record rather than its own motion, in `profile.py compare` and in the fit alike (`measure_note`'s and `analyze_note`'s `tnr_mask`, `probe_rows`' `tnr_masks`). Measured through its own, a still model whose partials carried the reference's skirt read that skirt as noise its bare-lobe mask left outside, while a model given a vibrato the reference lacked widened its own mask and read cleaner the more it moved. A reference row without the record is refused rather than read as still. The `tnr` term and the gate both read the reference's mask over the gate's fit window: a reference row records it as `fit_tnr_mask` (the same `fm` and `am` record, from `measure_tnr_mask` over the fit window), the reference's ratio is read through it, and the model's through the reference's. `tnr_mask` stays the sustain-window record `analyze_note` reads; the two windows' masks move the reading by up to 29.1 dB per note on lead_bass_lead_module, 26.6 dB on synth_brass_2_module and 22.2 dB on tremolo_strings, and by more than 0.1 dB on 55 of the 114 captures whose gate bounds `tnr`.

The same motion reading decides the pitch and the partial stack, in the fit's `analyze_note` and the reference profile's `find_partials` alike. Under a pitch motion the spectral peak is whichever FM line won and the upper partials' peaks fall with the modulation index, so under a vibrato the fundamental (`f0_hz`, `f0_cents_err`, `cents_vs_et`) is the mean pitch of the fundamental demodulated under its own FM extent (a wider passband lets a component beside the fundamental pull the mean). Without a vibrato the fundamental is `metrics_partials.line_centroid_pitch`: each partial's power centroid over the partial's own Hann lobe plus 15 cents either side, divided by its place in the series (stretched by the fitted stiffness) and averaged by power across the first twelve partials. Detuned oscillators, unison strings, a celeste rank or a layered patch put two or three lines a few cents apart under every partial, and a peak picks whichever the window's beat phase favoured: a sawtooth lead's reference read −4.2 to +4.2 cents from note to note against a centre within 0.8 of equal temperament. The upper partials, whose lines separate, steady the low ones, whose lines do not resolve inside the window. A partial whose strongest line sits past the 15 cents holds another mode (a membrane's next mode beside a stretched prediction) and is left out. The reading is the power-weighted mean pitch over the window, the quantity a vibrato's demodulated track is averaged to, so a note that glides reads its mean and a single still line reads where its peak did. Under either motion each partial level (`harmonics_db`, `partials_db`) is its power summed over the same FM reach the tone mask uses.

A still partial's level is the power over its Hann lobe centred on the peak found within 25 cents of the series, which does not scallop with the peak's offset from its bin; under a motion the lobe centres on the series. Against a 16x zero-padded Blackman-Harris peak read of the same segment it agrees within about 1 dB on a narrow line within 40 dB of h1, where a bare peak reads up to 1.42 dB low; a line that chorus or detuning has broadened reads its power, up to about 3 dB above that peak. A partial standing only a few dB over the note's own floor between partials, read as the same lobe's power in the valley midway to the next partial, carries that floor in its level, and the lobe sum takes 1 to 5 dB more of it than a bare peak; the window's leakage from h1 lies 150 dB or more under h1 at those distances and plays no part. `ladder_present`'s fixed 80 dB margin does not see that floor, so such a partial near the margin enters or leaves `balance` on a change of a dB or two. Each statistic of a versioned dimension records in `set_reader` the reader version its value was set under (`profile_measure.READER_VERSIONS`); a bound another version set is re-recorded as `rewindowed` only where the new reading exceeds it and the last re-record found it held, and otherwise keeps ratcheting.

The independent profile axes are [`ToneClass`](../toneclass.py), [`ExcitationFamily`](../toneclass.py), and the keyboard interface. A `FitProfile` combines those axes; it does not infer one axis from another. The current classes are `sustained`, `struck-string`, `plucked-string`, `modal`, and `noise`; the excitation families are `bowed`, `plucked`, `hammered`, `struck-modal`, `percussion`, `breath-onset` (the free-reed, brass and flute engines, whose breath or bellows onset the engine shapes), and `other`.

Stringlike classes may use a harmonic ladder and stiffness; modal classes use measured modes found in the signal. A modal voice has no harmonic ladder requirement. `modal_hz` and `modal_db` describe measured partials, and the `modes` term matches those partials when the reference provides them. `modes` belongs to the modal classes and to percussion: on a harmonic voice both sides fill the peak list with the first harmonics, `harm` already prices them, and the diagnosis omits the term unless `--w-modes` is given. `ToneClass.stringlike` also decides the gate: the four dimensions read off the n·f0 ladder (`stretch`, `balance`, `partial_tilt`, `tnr`) are canonical only for a stringlike class, so a modal capture neither asks for them nor holds a bound on them, and its `dimensions_na` records what each would read on that reference.

Pitch and envelope windows are bounded by the analyzed note duration. `attack_ms` remains score anchored; fine attack and modulation fields are additional probes. A silent or non-comparable row is unscorable rather than a zero measurement.

A reference profile's per-timbre summary records `velocity_law`, the source's level-to-velocity law read off `held_peak_dbfs` (the held body rather than the strike, whose per-note transient widens the spread). For each adjacent pair of grid velocities v1 < v2, every note's step is the level difference and its exponent is k = step / (20 log10(v2/v1)), so gain is proportional to velocity^k; across notes each pair carries the median and interquartile range of both. The timbre reads `mapping` when every pair's step IQR is within 0.25 dB and `varies by note` otherwise; a pair on which no note moves by more than 0.1 dB is `flat`. A pair needs at least three notes, and a timbre with no such pair (one velocity, or too few notes) has no reading. On a reference that reads as a mapping the velocity-to-level steps are the source product's gain table, not the instrument's acoustics, so `vel_range` there measures that table rather than a playing dynamic. `profile.py compare` prints the law beside `vel_range` as `velocity law (held_peak_dbfs): mapping k=<median>, spread <x> dB (IQR k <y>)` or `velocity law (held_peak_dbfs): varies by note, spread <x> dB`, with `flat at v<v1>-v<v2>` added where it applies; k is the median over the non-flat pairs and summarises the curve (the per-pair values are the record), the spread is the widest pair's step IQR the class is decided on, and the IQR of k is that widest pair's across-note IQR of k; the fit's gate audit prints the same line per capture whose `vel_range` it read. A profile measured before the field existed has the law computed from its rows, and the line says `(computed, not recorded)`. The reading changes no bound, gate field or loss term.

## Percussion measurements

### Fields

| group | current fields | meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| identity and bands | `note`, `velocity`, `bands_db`, `peak_band_hz`, `band_decay_db_s` | Hit coordinates, loudness-normalized one-third-octave bands, peak band, and octave-band decay rates. |
| onset and envelope | `onset_ms`, `attack_ms`, `attack_floored`, `decay_ms`, `decay_capped`, `crest_db`, `level_db` | Transient timing, floor/cap provenance, crest, and normalized hit level. |
| spectral shape | `centroid_hz`, `flatness_db`, `tilt_rise_db`, `tilt_strike_db` | Spectrum, flatness, and body-versus-strike tilt; `bright` is a loss term derived from the measured spectrum. |
| tone and modes | `tone_f0_hz`, `tone_lowest_hz`, `modal_hz`, `modal_db`, `modal_ratio`, `pitch_drop_ratio`, `pitch_drop_ms` | Strongest/lowest mode and mode ratios; pitch drop is measured data and may be represented through the mode terms. `tonal` is a loss term derived from flatness. |
| stereo and texture | `stereo_width`, `modal_density`, `prompt_late_db`, `evolution_db`, `window_flatness_db` | Stereo width, octave-cell density/prompt measures, and early spectral evolution. |
| masks and diagnostics | `modal_density_valid`, `prompt_late_valid`, `evolution_valid`, `evolution_measured`, `window_flatness_valid`, `window_flatness_measured` | Domain eligibility and finite geometry validity. Genuinely unmeasured slots use placeholders; below-floor measured slots retain finite values even when reference eligibility is false. `ring` is a loss derived from decay. |

The ordinary percussion profile uses one-third-octave centers from 50 Hz through 12.5 kHz and octave decay centers from 63 Hz through 8 kHz. `band_edge_hz` is capture/profile metadata, not a hit-row field. A reference-derived band edge and the measured geometry determine which cells can participate; the profile does not promise energy beyond that edge.

`evolve` uses three windows and eight bands (20–60 Hz plus the seven texture bands) over a common untapered 0–240 ms span: 0–15 ms, 15–60 ms, and 60–240 ms; `diffuse` uses the same windows for 1–8 kHz window flatness. The first window uses a falling half-Hann; later windows use Hann. Power is Parseval-normalized and bandwidth-limited. Actual unpadded FFT bins control eligibility; zero padding interpolates the spectrum and does not resolve slow, low-frequency bands. A low band also needs two cycles and two actual bins.

The relative `-60 dB` threshold is a reference-domain eligibility rule, not a measured SNR. `*_valid` identifies reference-eligible cells; `*_measured` identifies finite candidate geometry even below that floor. A finite candidate below the reference floor is compared, while an absent candidate is charged by the loss contract. This prevents a threshold cliff.

## Loss and weights

### Terms

| scope | current terms |
| --- | --- |
| shared | `env`, `mss`, `level`, `crest`, `dyn`, `modes`, `bright`, `velspan`, `velcurve` |
| pitched | `harm`, `cents`, `tnr`, `mod`, `init`, `slope`, `tail`, `hf`, `hfshare`, `lf`, `stiff`, `hfdyn`, `modedyn`, `onsetvel`, `onset`, `arrival`, `body` |
| percussion | `band`, `bandrms`, `bdecay`, `tilt`, `tonal`, `rise`, `strike`, `ring`, `lf`, `kit`, `density`, `prompt`, `evolve`, `diffuse` |

A gated dimension's own term (`GATE_OWN_TERMS`, `PERCUSSION_GATE_OWN_TERMS` in `loss_weights.py`) reads each note through the function the gate reads that dimension with, listed per term in `loss.OWN_TERM_READERS` and `loss.PERCUSSION_OWN_TERM_READERS`; `test_own_term_readers.py` holds each term's per-note reading equal to the gate's on one note.

| gated dimension | own term | reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stretch` | `cents` | `cents_vs_et`, less the reference's `a4_offset_c` |
| `tnr` | `tnr` | `fit_tnr_db` |
| `centroid_pct` | `bright` (pitched) | `fit_centroid_hz` |
| `vel_range` | `velspan` | `mono_peak_dbfs` |
| `register` | `level` (pitched) | `held_peak_dbfs` |
| `attack_hf` | `hfshare` | the 4-8 kHz band's median of `attack_hf_db` |
| `attack_hf_swing` | `hfdyn` | the same, softest to hardest |
| `onset`, `onset_settle` | `onset` | `onset_overshoot_db`, `onset_settle_ms` |
| `body` | `body` | `body_below_f0_db` |
| `crest` (drum) | `crest` | `crest_db` |
| `band_shape` (drum) | `bandrms` | `bands_db` |
| `centroid_pct` (drum) | `bright` | `centroid_hz` |
| `tonality` (drum) | `tonal` | `flatness_db` |
| `tilt_rise` (drum) | `rise` | `tilt_rise_db` |

The term reads what the gate reads, from the same input. A pitched note is read off the mean downmix of the capture whatever `--mono-mode` says, through the fit window the gate places (to the loop's `read_to_join_s` on a looped reference row, which the model's row takes from its reference's), with the onset's envelope window set by the fit's f0. A kit's bands are read up to the committed profile's `band_edge_hz`, and the whole range where the profile records none; only a capture with no profile takes the edge its own oracle measures. `velspan` compares both sides' spans over the velocities they share, and reads nothing where they share fewer than two. The reference's `a4_offset_c` is the profile's `stretch_ruler` over the capture definition's grid and the fit's program.

A reading the gate leaves out of a reference row is left out of the term on that row: the loop's exclusions (`loop_unweighted`, which now includes `body`) and the recording's floor (`floor_censored`, `tnr` and `body`, decided by `tnr_floor_state` and `body_floor_state`). `tnr_floor_dropped` and `body_floor_dropped` count the readings the floor took, and the fit's `write_back` record carries them as `floor_dropped`; a term left with no reading at all is listed under `not_computed` and is dropped from the weights (`DROP_REASONS`) rather than scoring zero. An absent model reading never costs less than a present one: `tnr`, `cents` and `hfshare` cap a present delta at the cost of an absent reading.

Every other mapping is a proxy: `stiff` reads B, `hf` the attack's five bands slice by slice, a pitched `crest` peak over held RMS, `band` each band capped with the reference's floored bands skipped.

`bright` is the gate's centroid ratio, |100 (model / reference − 1)| per note, counted in `bright_hits`; a pitched note reads `fit_centroid_hz`, a hit `centroid_hz`. It weighs 1 on percussion and 0 on a pitched voice, where breath and bow noise move it note by note; a pitched fit asks for it with `--w-bright`, which is what sees a string that has slipped into a higher regime — a missing fundamental in the bass costs `harm` little under audibility weighting and doubles the centroid.

`cents` is the gate's `stretch` per note: |model − reference `cents_vs_et` + the reference's `a4_offset_c`|, so a reference whose own A4 sits off 440 Hz charges a model tuned to 440 nothing; a model with no reading where the reference has one is charged 50 cents. `stiff` is not a reading of `stretch`: its B is fitted on `analyze_note`'s sustain window by `estimate_inharmonicity_b`,, a different window and fit from the gate's `read_partials`, so the two do not read the same B. `tnr` is |model − reference| `fit_tnr_db` per note, two-sided as the gate's bound is: a model measurably cleaner than a sampled reference is usually one missing the reference's own movement, so it is charged as a noisier one is. A model with no reading where the reference has one is charged 24 dB, and `tnr_notes` counts the notes compared. `hfshare` is |model − reference| of the gate's `attack_hf` reading per note, a missing model reading charged the attack bands' 24 dB cap and counted in `hfshare_notes`. `bandrms` is the gate's `band_shape`, the RMS distance of the two band profiles over every band, uncapped, counted in `bandrms_hits`. On a pitched probe `level` reads `held_peak_dbfs`, the gate's `register` reading; a drum's reads `held_rms_dbfs`. A drum's `crest` reads the gate's `crest_db`, a pitched voice's `held_crest_db`.

`arrival` is the gate's `attack` dimension per note: |model − reference| `arrival_ms`, the time from the onset to the first point within 3 dB of the note's own maximum, read over the note to its note-off, counted in `arrival_hits` and scaled by 40 ms. `env` reads its attack as a 10–90 % rise inside the first half-second, which a level still climbing after that does not move; a string that creeps up 3 dB over two seconds matches `env` and arrives 650 ms late on the gate. It weighs 0 by default; `--w-arrival` opts in.

`body` is the gate's `body` dimension per note: |model − reference| `body_below_f0_db`, the 40 Hz to 0.7 f0 band against the fundamental's, from 0.2 to 1.2 s after the onset and only from a 120 Hz fundamental up, capped at 24 dB and counted in `body_hits`. A string bowed under its minimum force slips more than once a period and loses its fundamental, which reads tens of dB here while `harm` prices it lightly. It weighs 0 by default; `--w-body` opts in.

`dyn` and `modedyn` share one reducer, a per-pitch slope of a brightness reading against velocity, and differ in the reading: `dyn` the held ladder's h4–h10, `modedyn` the share of measured mode power at 1.75× the played note and above, floored at the mode detector's 55 dB. `hfdyn` is the gate's `attack_hf_swing`: per pitch, the model's change in the attack's 4–8 kHz share from the softest to the hardest velocity both sides read, minus the reference's, as an absolute value capped at 24 dB. It is an endpoint change and not a fitted slope, since a law that rises and then falls has a rising slope and a falling end; a model with no change to read where the reference has one is charged the cap. `onsetvel` takes the same per-pitch slope of `attack_fine_ms` and `onset_overshoot_db` instead, so it reads how the onset changes with velocity; it weighs 0 by default, and a reference whose velocity rows share one onset cannot answer it. `velspan` is the term for the gate's `vel_range`: per pitch, the absolute difference between the model's and the reference's softest-to-hardest `mono_peak_dbfs` span over the velocities both hold, capped at the level cap, with a model span missing where the reference has one charged the cap. It weighs 0.5 by default in the classes that default `dyn` or `modedyn`. `velcurve` reads `peak_dbfs` across each pitch's whole velocity ladder after removing that pitch's common model-minus-reference offset (the capped-L1 optimum, the median on ties), so it sees a wrong middle-velocity response that a matching span hides; a model peak missing where the reference has one is charged the level cap, and the robust aggregate reports its upper tail and worst note. It weighs 0 by default; `--w-velcurve` opts in. Both terms, and every velocity-axis term, need a per-pitch velocity span of at least 16. The gate's `onset` and `onset_settle` dimensions (model minus reference `onset_overshoot_db` and `onset_settle_ms`, rows absent on either side skipped) are read by the `onset` term. `onset` compares the onset itself cell by cell: the overshoot's difference in dB plus the settle's in doublings, with the settle offset by 10 ms so a zero settle stays finite. A cell either side reads onset-ineligible is left out and counted by side (`onset_ref_ineligible`, `onset_model_ineligible`), never scored as a match. It weighs 1 for the `breath-onset` family and 0 otherwise, and it is dropped, or refused when named, on a capture whose committed profile has no eligible cell on the probe's grid. The modal class weights `modedyn` and `mod` in place of `dyn` and `tnr`; `mod` tracks the fundamental, so it reads a vibraphone's vanes and a beating pair at the fundamental, not a pair among the upper modes.

The authoritative term definitions, caps, and reducers are in [`loss.py`](../loss.py), [`loss_dimensions.py`](../loss_dimensions.py), and [`loss_weights.py`](../loss_weights.py). Losses use fixed engineering units recorded by `TERM_UNITS`, not perceptual units: pitch is cents, spectral and level residuals are dB-like engineering quantities, envelope and decay terms retain their declared time/rate units, and texture terms retain their cell residual units. One quality unit means one declared engineering unit for the aggregate term; it does not mean perceptual equivalence.

Pitched harmonic, modal, pitch, noise, envelope, and modulation terms compare the finite cells appropriate to their measurement. Percussion band, decay, tilt, tone, ring, kit, density, prompt, evolution, and diffuse terms use their corresponding cells. `mss` compares the whole timeline. Level terms first remove the median model/reference gain offset, so a common gain does not turn normalized shape agreement into an absolute-level claim. Raw held levels are scored only when both sides provide the raw fields.

The bank's relative program levels are set from that removed offset, taken at each program's own reference, and from its median across the GM programs: each included voice's level offset against its own reference is brought to the bank's median offset, by its patch `gain` alone. A voice is included when its offset sits more than 2 dB from that median. A boost stops at a gain of 8.0, and at the gain where an eight-note chord at full velocity would peak above the higher of 0 dBFS and its peak before the change. A voice is left out when its reference is captured with its rig baked in, holds fewer than five pitches, has no gate, or spreads its per-note offsets over more than 6 dB p10–p90, because a gain cannot correct a register shape. Programs 80–95 keep the balance of their module references. A machine_defined program whose governing (module) and rack references differ by 3 dB or more in held-window drop (the level 0.2–0.4 s after onset minus 1.0–1.2 s, median over held notes), or whose drop cannot be read on them, is not adjudicable on the rack level ruler: it is left out of the median and never moved. Before the 2 dB line is applied to voices already brought to the median, the shift they share is removed, since a shift every moved voice shares is a bank-wide trim rather than a balance; a voice moves only where its own remainder exceeds the 0.48 dB note-order repeatability floor of the equal-power held-level read. A boost is checked against a chord peak read at the voice's current gain. One assumption underlies the whole: the references are assumed to share one capture level; this is consistent with their levels and not recorded.

Evolution and diffuse reducers retain the cell mean and the worst-quarter cell mean, with at least one cell in the quarter; the aggregate keeps the larger residual. The robust grid reducer separately reports mean, upper-tail, worst row, and note/velocity provenance. It does not report or independently gate every individual cell. Grid terms such as `mss`, `dyn`, `hfdyn`, and `kit` keep their declared grid reducer.

Reference-unknown data supplies no target. Fixed reference-cell reducers charge missing candidates at the cap; other count-tracked terms protect losses against disappearing measurements through anchored coverage penalties in `LossWeights`. `CellCount` records compared, clipped, absent, and skipped cells; `<term>_capped` includes clipped plus absent cells. Each tallied term also reports `<term>_uncapped`, the same reducer with every clipped cell charged its full distance (an absent cell stays at the cap), and the grid reducer adds `<term>_uncapped_worst`; no loss or quality number reads them. For evolution and diffuse, ineligible reference cells remain outside the denominator and are reported separately as `evolution_reference_unavailable` and `window_flatness_reference_unavailable`. They do not increment `<term>_skipped`.

Weights are resolved in this order: explicit CLI weight, specification weight, then the `FitProfile`/class defaults in [`toneclass.py`](../toneclass.py) and [`loss_weights.py`](../loss_weights.py). The exact resolved values belong in the current fit/report output; this page does not copy a default numeric weight table. A normalized loss is relative to its starting weighted score; `--raw-loss` keeps the raw weighted sum. Neither display is a perceptual score.

### Quality units

[`fit_quality.py`](../fit_quality.py) reports absolute weighted residuals in the fixed term units and uses one declared unit as the default target for each aggregate and each reported worst row/condition. A term meets that numerical target only with complete known coverage and both residuals at most one declared unit. Unknown or zero anchored counts, lost measurements, and capped, absent, or skipped evidence prevent complete known coverage. Use [`report.py`](../report.py) for resolved weights and quality fields; [Acceptance](acceptance.md#numerical-quality) defines how to read them.

## Coverage

Coverage is assessed against the anchored reference domain and counts. Missing candidate geometry stays charged and does not shrink that domain. Pitched modal and harmonic domains are independent: a missing modal reference does not invent a harmonic target, and a modal candidate is not judged by a harmonic ladder. Percussion edge and texture masks distinguish reference eligibility from finite measured candidate data.

Fit quality must identify the active terms, compared/capped/absent/skipped counts, aggregate residual, worst row/condition, and whether all required dimensions were measured. Use [`reference.md`](reference.md) for capture and probe procedures, [`fitting.md`](fitting.md) for the CLI workflow, and [`development.md`](development.md) for recovery and probe work; those pages own procedures rather than this measurement contract.
