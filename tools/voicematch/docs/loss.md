# Loss — the number a fit minimises

`loss.py`. Built from the same fields `report.json` carries ([metrics.md](metrics.md)). Every term has a `--w-<name>` flag; what a flag means is below, and what a run uses when you name none is at [the foot of this page](#a-weight-nobody-gave-comes-from-what-the-instrument-is).

## Per-note terms

- **harmonic profile** (`--w-harm`) — L1 distance of the h1-normalized `harmonics_db` over the first `--n-harm` harmonics, which reaches the whole ladder `analyze_note` measures, the most directly actionable timbre signal — for a voice that has a harmonic series. A bin where **neither** side found a partial is skipped rather than charged, read against each side's own loudest bin rather than against the `-120 dB` sentinel, and `harm_bins` reports how many bins above the fundamental survived on both sides. Zero there is not a match, it is the term saying this instrument has no ladder where the ladder looks; weight `--w-modes` instead. Each partial's vote is scaled by how audible it is — an A-weighting at its own frequency and a masking slope against the loudest partial of the same note — so A0's 27.5 Hz fundamental no longer outvotes the partials that carry its timbre, and a 10 dB error on something 50 dB down is no longer charged in full. `--flat-partial-weighting` restores equal votes.
- **measured partials** (`--w-modes`) — the partials as found, paired against the reference's nearest-first by frequency and priced in cents and dB, with an unpaired mode on either side charged more than any mistuning can cost, so a fit cannot delete a partial it is unable to place. This is the only pitched term a bar, a bell, a plate or a membrane has, and it is what a drum's pitch is scored by.
- **movement** (`--w-mod`) — vibrato depth and rate, tremolo, the slow beat of an ensemble or a unison pair, and how wide the fundamental is. It exists because a model reading "cleaner, flatter" than its oracle usually needs vibrato, while the objective had no term that could ever say so: `tnr` charges the model only for being *noisier* and is silent when it is dead. An absent reading on the model side where the reference has one is charged rather than skipped, since a note too still to track is the defect. **A rate is skipped on the reference's depth alone and never on the model's**, for the same reason and with a sharper edge: the parts are summed, so a dropped rate part is a straight subtraction, and requiring both sides to clear the depth floor put a cliff in the term that rewarded exactly what it exists to charge. Against a reference at 20 cents and 2 dB, a model at 5.1 cents and 1.1 dB scored 10.88 and one at 4.9 and 0.9 — dimmer on both axes — scored 4.12. A decaying note reads as still by construction, since the level track is detrended before the modulation spectrum is taken, so a fit could buy the discount by letting the note die. Every weighted voice in the bank was taking it: closing the gate raised `mod` on 23 of 34 rendered probes, by 1.28x to 3.10x, and moved no other term on any of them.
- **intonation** (`--w-cents`) — absolute f0 cents difference from the oracle.
- **noise floor** (`--w-tnr`) — TNR shortfall, penalised **only when the model is noisier** than the oracle (a model cleaner than the sampled oracle, which carries natural vibrato/breath, is not penalised). Being one-sided, its zero has two opposite meanings — every note matched, or the model is cleaner than the reference everywhere and the term has run out of anything to say — so `tnr_notes` reports how many notes it actually charged for. Read it before believing a zero: on a three-note piano probe the term reached exactly 0.00 on ten of forty-six candidates and the winner was one of them, which on a normalised objective is a full unit of loss bought by overshooting into "cleaner". Widening the probe to seven notes stopped it reaching zero at all.
- **temporal envelope** (`--w-env`) — sustain slope, release and attack differences: the ring-down signature a purely spectral match is blind to (attack / decay / crest for a drum). The attack measures are windowed from the note's *detected* onset rather than its scheduled one, so a model that speaks 30 ms late is not scored as a model with a different spectrum; the delay itself is carried on the row as `onset_ms` rather than discarded.
- **skeleton** (`--w-init` / `--w-slope`) — per-harmonic onset ladder and decay slopes, which separate the *excitation* spectrum from the *loop* decay that the time-averaged harmonic term conflates.

## Percussion terms

- **band profile** (`--w-band`) — the 1/3-octave `bands_db` distance: the percussion analogue of the harmonic ladder.
- **band decay** (`--w-bdecay`) — the per-octave decay slopes, compared as a **ratio** rather than as a difference in dB/s, one unit being a factor of two. A difference cannot be scaled: the same estimator returns −20 dB/s for a tom's shell and −800 for the stick click on top of it, both correctly, so a cap wide enough for the second saturates on everything and one narrow enough for the first saturates on the second.
- **low-end balance** (`--w-lf`) — see below.
- **kit relations** (`--w-kit`) — how far the model's *kit-internal* relations sit from the reference's, for the families a capture declares in its `groups` block (the six toms, the three hi-hats, the seven cymbals). Every other term compares one hit at a time, so a kit whose every piece is individually plausible can still have its toms out of order — the first thing a drummer hears. Four relations are read in doublings (`pitch` from `tone_f0_hz`, `decay`, `colour` from `centroid_hz`, `level` from `peak_dbfs`), per velocity, since two members struck at different velocities are not in a relation. Which of the four count is **measured, not declared**: a relation is scored only where the reference's own members sit at least `KIT_MIN_SPREAD` (0.25 doublings) apart. This kit's six toms are level-matched to within 1.2 dB — 0.19 doublings — so there is no level relation among them to reproduce and scoring one would charge the model for the reference's strike-to-strike variation, while the same group's pitch spans 1.37 doublings and its decay 0.98, which are relations. It scores 0 with no groups, and needs at least `KIT_MIN_MEMBERS` (2) members present at a velocity.

**A family is a set, and what is compared is its sorted contrasts.** Each member is centred on its own family's median and the two sorted vectors are compared, so where the family as a whole sits cancels — that part is already priced by `modes`, `env` and `level` — and what is left is the spread and the spacing. Two consequences, both wanted. It needs no note map and cannot charge for one: this kit lays its six toms out as 45, 47, 48, 50, 41, 43 against GM's order, and a set has no order to disagree about. And it is blind to a **permuted** family — a model with the open hi-hat's decay on the closed key scores here exactly as one with them the right way round. That is the division of labour: placement is what a per-note term is good at, and `env` charges a swapped hi-hat pair some eight units a row, while spread is what a per-note term loses the moment it saturates.

**The saturation is the argument.** The reference's six toms run 71 to 184 Hz and libsonare's key-track from 100 to 167, so every one of the six is more than `MODE_CENTS_CAP` out and `modes` returns its cap on all six. Widening the series — the one repair that would fix it — changes that term by nothing at all until the members come back under the cap. A saturated term is not a weak signal, it is no signal, and a contrast is not capped by the same arithmetic. `KIT_DOUBLING_CAP` is 3.0 for the same reason: tight enough to bind on an ordinary error would reintroduce exactly what this escapes.

**Two guards worth knowing.** A member whose *reference* decay hit the analysis ceiling is dropped from the `decay` relation — that number is the window, not the instrument — while a capped **model** decay is kept, because it is a lower bound on a render that rang past the window and a charge from a lower bound can only understate. And `--drum-note N` narrows the grid to that one note unless `--notes` says otherwise, so the default drum fit has no family in it: the class default turns itself off there rather than scoring 0.0, and an explicit `--w-kit` is refused with the reason.

The term is a mean, so it dilutes — the shipped kit resolves to 790 member-relations across thirteen families. Neither reader is hurt by that: a fit names the family it is working on (`--notes 41,43,45,47,48,50`) and then the term *is* that family, and `profile.py compare` prints `loss.kit_report`, one line per family and relation with the reference's spread beside the model's, which never pools families at all.

**`lf` prices the low end as one region.** `band` is a mean over twenty-five bands, so a kick 27 dB hot at 50 Hz moves it by about one unit — nothing, against a term whose typical value is in the tens — while being the single most audible error a kit can have. `lf` reads the bands at or below 160 Hz against everything above them and charges the difference between that balance and the reference's. A **balance** rather than a level, because both profiles are normalised to their own loudest band and on a kick the loudest band *is* the bottom: comparing the low bands directly compares each side's anchor against itself and can only return zero. `level` remains the term for loudness, and a hit 12 dB louder scores nothing here.

## The whole-render term

- **multi-scale STFT** (`--w-mss`) — magnitude distance at four FFT sizes (512/1024/2048/4096), log and linear, with each bin weighted by 1/f so every **octave** contributes equally. This is what sees everything the metric set does not model: inharmonicity, structure between the harmonics, attack detail, the tail past the sustain window. Phase is ignored, since two renders of the same note are never phase-aligned. An unweighted mean over bins is a mean over the wrong measure — at 48 kHz half the bins sit above 12 kHz, where a sampled reference is mostly reporting its own capture bandwidth, and a handful cover the octave from 100 to 200 Hz where a bass note's identity lives. It is also the scale `shape/` already compares its spectrograms on, so the two halves of the harness no longer read the same renders through different frequency scales. It is level-blind, like every other term here: the two renders reach it scaled to a common RMS, which equalises *total energy* and so lifts a model that decays faster than its reference, and the ladder and band terms cancel that lift by being ratios while this one did not — a gain alone charged 0.80 at 1.5× and 4.06 at 4× before the gain where both sides still sound was divided out.

## Seven terms for what a shape normalises away

Everything above is a *shape*, normalised past the very things a listener notices first.

- **aftersound** (`--w-tail`) — the per-harmonic decay slope 2–6 s in. It has frames to fit only when the probe holds a note that long, which is why it belongs with `--corpus`: on a piano the aftersound is most of the note, and a model whose C6 fell to nothing in 1.6 s of an 8 s hold used to score identically to one that held for eleven, because both were measured over the same first two seconds.
- **crest** (`--w-crest`) — peak minus held RMS, per note. A difference of two levels from one render, so any gain common to both cancels and it needs no calibration at all. It is the only term that can fail on a note whose envelope never falls after its attack: a model 3.9 dB over the reference at the peak and 8.7 dB over in held RMS is not loud, it is not decaying, and every normalised term says it is correct.
- **level balance** (`--w-level`) — how loud each note is relative to the others, with the grid's own median offset removed first. Absolute dBFS is not comparable between a model rendered here and a reference captured through somebody else's output stage, and a term that treated it as comparable would spend the fit's budget on an output gain. The removed offset is printed once per run, since a model uniformly 9 dB over its reference is worth knowing about even though no voicing knob should be the one to fix it. `--max-level-drift-db` (default 6.0) bounds how far the winner may move the whole-grid level away from the start point before the loss charges it.
- **attack tilt** (`--w-hf`) — the high-band balance in 20 ms slices over the first 120 ms, each band relative to that slice's own broadband level. Level-blind by construction, and it catches what a whole-timeline distance averages away: a 43 ms excess of +53 dB at 20–24 kHz on a note whose midband matches within half a decibel is a tick, and the ear finds it instantly.
- **attack weight** (`--w-lf`, pitched voices) — the same window's other end: 20 Hz to 4 kHz, in five bands over one 50 ms window rather than six slices, because at the bottom of the range frequency resolution is worth more than time resolution (20–60 Hz and 60–200 Hz are one bin and three at a 20 ms slice's resolution, so they cannot be separated at all). Averaged over the bands the *reference* offered rather than summed over them, so that this name and its percussion branch — a mean of one balance per hit — carry the same aggregate and one perceptual unit can scale both; summed, the two were five times apart. The register that needs it is the bass. An excitation with no highpass of its own puts the attack's energy below where the instrument radiates, and that reads as a note which is felt and never heard — an A1 carrying 43 dB more 20–60 Hz than its reference while sitting 15–20 dB under it above 200 Hz. Nothing else here can see it: the ladder is h1-normalised, so an excess at or under the fundamental is invisible by construction, and every other pitched term reads the sustain window, which a 30 ms strike event is over before it opens.

  Each band is read against a 200 Hz – 4 kHz anchor rather than against the window's own total. A share of the total is compositional, so a defect confined to one band fabricates a delta in every other: measured on a band-limited 40 Hz excess, the three bands from 200 Hz up each reported 4.92 dB that was not there. Against the anchor they report exactly zero. `--w-hf` keeps the share of the total, and that asymmetry is the window rather than a preference — its 20 ms slice is shorter than one cycle of a 40 Hz excess, so the leakage lands in the anchor and the same fix inverts.
- **string stiffness** (`--w-stiff`) — how far the model stretches its twelfth partial against how far the reference does, in cents. It exists because correcting the ladder removed the only thing that priced stiffness at all: a model stiffer than its reference used to show up as tens of decibels of fabricated harmonic error, which was the wrong quantity in the wrong term but at least a pressure in the right direction. With the ladder measured along each string's own series, a voice twice as stiff as the reference scores a clean sheet unless this is weighted. Counted only on notes where both sides fitted at least `MIN_PARTIALS_FOR_B` partials; unlike the dynamics term a zero here is a real match, since two harmonic instruments genuinely agree that neither stretches.
- **dynamics curve** (`--w-dyn`) — how brightness tracks velocity, fitted per pitch so register is held fixed. The only term that lives in the relation BETWEEN notes; every other one compares a note at a time and averages, which prices the size of a per-note error but not its trend. Two candidates with identical per-note ladder error, one tracking the reference's response to force and one inverted, score the same `harm` and are told apart only here. It needs a probe with a velocity axis (`--pattern velocity`, or a drum probe) and the run refuses the weight otherwise, because its unmeasurable value is 0.0 and 0.0 is also its best score.

## Every partial term searches the string's own series

A stiff string puts its nth partial at `n·f0·√(1 + B·n²)`, and B is estimated per note from the low partials, where the search window still contains them. It matters more than its size suggests. At B=4e-4 — an ordinary grand's bass — the twelfth partial is 48 cents sharp, outside the ±40 cent window, and used to read 49 dB dark; at 8e-4 the ninth through twelfth read 66 to 86 dB dark. The `tnr` mask runs to the fortieth partial, where the same stiffness moves it 427 cents, and filed 40 dB of real partial energy as noise. Both terms carry weight 1.0 by default for a struck string, so this was in every fit ever run against a piano.

The failure is a cliff, not a gradient: a partial is inside the window or it is not, so a model and a reference whose stiffness differs slightly land on opposite sides and produce a mismatch of tens of decibels that is a property of the ruler. B is reported per note as `inharmonicity_b` and is deliberately **not** scored — a stiffness difference is real and worth seeing, but it belongs to the string's physics rather than to the timbre terms, which are now measured independently of it. A voice whose partials are harmonic estimates B as exactly zero and reads bit-for-bit what it read before any of this existed.

## A term that stops being measurable is charged, not credited

Every averaged term above skips the data points it cannot use and divides by the survivors, which is right until there are none: the sum is then 0.0 over 0 points, and 0.0 is that term's best possible score. A render whose modes vanished, whose vibrato stopped being trackable or whose partials fell under the floor reads as the render that *fixed* the term, and a search takes that trade every time it is offered.

Each term already reported its own count — `harm_bins`, `modes_notes`, `mod_notes`, `stiff_notes`, `dyn_groups`, `band_bins`, `bdecay_bins`, `lf_notes` — and nothing read them. `LossWeights` now records those counts at the start point and charges twice its start value for any term that had data there and has none here. A term that never had data (a probe with no velocity axis cannot score `dyn`) is not charged for still not having it, and `--raw-loss` never calibrates, so the guard needs a start point and stays out of the way without one.

**The other end of the same failure saturates instead of vanishing, so none of the guards above look for it.** A capped aggregate — `slope`, `init`, `tail`, `hf`, `lf`, `mod`, `modes`, `band`, `bdecay` — charges the cap both when the difference genuinely reached it and when the model had no value there at all, and the raw total cannot tell those apart from each other or from a real distance. A term made entirely of caps therefore reads as its *worst* value, which no empty-set test catches, and under a fixed perceptual scale its size is the cap rather than the distance. Measured across the bank's rendered probes: `slope` on two saxophone voices came to 24 cells with not one comparison among them, and one kit's `modes` to 72; 13 of the weighted (voice, term) pairs were half caps or more.

So each of those terms reports `<term>_cells` and `<term>_capped`, read exactly as `harm_bins` is, and `LossWeights.unreached` names the terms where the two are equal together with the share of the loss each carries. **Nothing is reweighted and no cell is dropped from a mean**: dropping the cells nothing could compare is precisely how an empty set comes to score as a match, which is what the paragraphs above exist to prevent. What changes is that a number made of caps can no longer be read as a measurement. The same accessor catches the opposite shape — a term whose reference offered no cells at all, which is how `tail` scores a perfect `0.0` on any probe shorter than the two-second mark where its band begins, on a voice whose class weights it.

This is the general form of the failure that made a silent render score a perfect harmonic ladder. `loss_terms` answers the ladder's version by refusing the whole comparison — returning unscorable when not one partial above the fundamental survived on both sides anywhere in the probe — which is right when the ladder *is* the objective and too blunt when one term of ten has gone quiet. Assume the same hole exists wherever a measurement is skipped and the rest averaged.

## Each term is divided by a fixed perceptual unit, never by its own start value

Without a scale the weights would not mean what they say: the harmonic term is an L1 sum over the ladder in dB and runs to tens, the intonation term is a handful of cents, the multi-scale term is a fraction, so weighting them in their own units hands whichever is numerically largest an influence nobody chose. The scale is `TERM_UNITS` — one difference anyone would notice, in that term's own raw units — and it is the same table `diagnose.py` divides a residual by to report "units", so a term the diagnosis calls matched at one unit is a term the fit charges one unit for.

**The scale must not be the term's value at the start point.** Dividing by what a term happened to measure at the start makes the pull of one raw unit `w / start`: the dimension that began furthest out is the one the objective is flattest along, and the dimension already nearly right is the one it is steepest along — the ear reacts to the largest error while the objective polishes the smallest. It also leaves the reported number with no absolute meaning. Measured across the bank's rendered probes, the start score under that scheme was the sum of the weights and nothing else on 10 of 34, and within one part in a thousand of it on the rest: a tenor sax whose note falls to digital zero inside its two-second hold and a trumpet that holds correctly both started at exactly `1.0`, and their distances from their references differ by a factor of six.

**A term that sums k cells carries the k in its unit.** Half the terms are per-note means of one number and half are sums over a ladder, a band profile or a slice grid. A unit written per cell hands every summing term an influence proportional to how many cells it aggregates — measured over the same probes, the summing terms came out at 40 to 400 units where the averaging ones came out at 1 to 15 — so one unit is one step on *every* cell of the aggregate, and each entry carries the count it was built from.

A second and separate layer divides the whole weighted sum by what the start point scored, so the start still reads exactly `1.0` and a reported `0.70` is 30 % better than the compiled-in values. It is one positive constant over every term, so it cannot move any term's pull relative to another's. `--raw-loss` skips both layers and restores unit weighting.

## A weight nobody gave comes from what the instrument is

Every `--w-*` flag defaults to `None` rather than to a number, and `toneclass.py` fills the gaps. It used to be one set of numbers for every instrument — `harm`, `cents` and `tnr` at one and everything else at zero — which scores a time-averaged spectrum, an intonation error and a noise floor and nothing else. That is defensible for a bowed note, where the spectrum genuinely is most of the identity, close to useless for a struck one, and actively wrong for a modal voice, where `harm` measures the noise floor.

`toneclass._CLASS_WEIGHTS`, as they stand:

| class | weights |
|---|---|
| `SUSTAINED` (bowed, blown) | `harm 1.0` `cents 0.5` `tnr 1.0` `mod 1.0` `env 0.5` `slope 0.5` |
| `STRUCK_STRING` | `harm 1.0` `cents 0.5` `tnr 0.5` `env 1.0` `init 1.0` `slope 1.0` `tail 1.0` `hf 0.5` `lf 0.5` `stiff 0.5` `crest 1.0` |
| `PLUCKED_STRING` | `harm 1.0` `cents 0.5` `tnr 0.5` `env 1.0` `init 1.5` `slope 1.0` `hf 0.5` `crest 1.0` `stiff 0.5` |
| `MODAL` (bar, bell, membrane) | `modes 1.0` `env 1.0` `init 1.0` `slope 1.0` `crest 1.0` `hf 0.5` — **`harm` deliberately absent** |
| `NOISE` | `mss 1.0` `env 1.0` `crest 1.0` |
| a drum probe (`PERCUSSION_WEIGHTS`) | `band 1.0` `bdecay 1.0` `env 1.0` `modes 0.5` `crest 0.5` `lf 1.0` `kit 1.0` |

The drum set is per-metric-set rather than per class: the percussion metric set produces `band` / `bdecay` and no ladder, whatever the kit piece is. Its `env` sits at 1.0 because the gesture is most of what tells two drums apart, where a sustained voice weights it at half that and the spectrum is the identity. A run prints the weights it resolved, so a default that depends on the instrument is never silent.

An explicit `--w-*` still wins, and a spec's `weights` block sits between the two — the class supplies only what neither names. A per-note weight inherited from the class is dropped on a probe with no analysis notes rather than refused, while one named on the command line is still refused, because asking for a measurement the probe cannot take is a mistake worth reporting and inheriting one is not the caller saying anything.

**A weight on a term the probe's metric set does not produce is dropped, and the run now names it.** `percussion_terms` computes `band` `bdecay` `env` `level` `crest` `dyn` `modes` `lf` `kit` `mss` and nothing else, so `--w-hf` on a drum fit multiplies a constant 0.0 — which is that term's best score — and two runs differing only in the flag come back byte-identical. Dropping it is right; dropping it in silence cost one drum round its belief that an axis had been weighted. Only what was asked for is reported, since a class default the probe cannot produce is not the caller saying anything either.

Putting the weights in the spec makes a fit reproducible from one file:

```json
{ "weights": { "harm": 1, "slope": 1, "tail": 2, "crest": 2, "level": 2, "hf": 1 },
  "knobs": [ { "tunable": "piano_voice.kTwoStageWidthOct", "min": 0.3, "max": 3.5 } ] }
```

`specs/piano_corpus.json` is the worked example. The bare-array spec form (a JSON list of knobs with no `weights` block) is unchanged.

## Whether a term is weighted is not whether it is measured

Every table above says what a term *prices*. What none of them says is how much of the objective a term actually moves, and the answer is not readable from the weights alone, because each term is divided by its own perceptual unit before the weight sees it. `make voicematch-loss-sensitivity` (`loss_sensitivity.py`) is how that is measured rather than reasoned about: it takes a captured reference, perturbs it by a named amount — raising partials 15-30, lengthening the attack, adding tremolo, negating the velocity response, adding hiss, cutting the output — and scores the result against the same capture's own timbre spread, so a reported 1.0 is the distance two of that instrument's references already sit apart and anything under it is a change the objective charges less for than picking the other reference.

Three properties are what make it an instrument rather than a demonstration.

**The perturbation is applied to audio, never to a knob.** A knob answers a different question — whether the knob reaches the defect — and a null reached through one cannot say which of the two was blind. Each perturbation is placed with the harness's own measurements of the render it is perturbing: partials go to `partial_hz(f0, k, B)`, the ramp starts at `note_onset`, the dynamics inversion is the negation of the slope `_brightness` itself reports, so the perturbation lands where the term looks rather than near it.

**Every perturbation carries an amplitude and is run at zero as well.** A zero-amplitude run that does not score exactly 0.0 is plumbing, and without it a number cannot be attributed to the perturbation at all. Each row also prints the size of the difference signal against the original and the row field the perturbation was supposed to move, so a loss that did not move is a finding only once the audio says it did — the two read identically in a results table otherwise.

**The yardstick is spread between references, and there is no tighter one in this tree.** These references are sampled plugins rendered deterministically: a second render is bit-identical, which `capture/drums.json` records as a checked property, so take-to-take variation is zero and cannot supply a scale. What a capture's several timbres bracket is chosen to be wide — two ends of a fingering choice, two registrations, two kits — so the denominator is an upper bound on how much the target wobbles and every ratio it produces is correspondingly a lower bound. Read a result under 1.0 as "cheaper than changing reference", not as "invisible".

What the run cannot say is that the loss agrees with a listener: no ranking by ear exists to check it against, and a positive control only ever supplies the necessary half — that a term responds to what it claims to price, and that the terms carrying no weight are measured and then discarded. `dyn`, `level`, and `mss` outside `NOISE` are the three that are computed on every row, reported on every run, and weighted at zero by every entry in `_CLASS_WEIGHTS` and by `PERCUSSION_WEIGHTS`.
